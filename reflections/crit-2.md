# Crit 2 Reflection

## 1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The biggest breakthrough came when I stopped thinking about the redesign as a visual refresh and instead treated it as an information architecture and usability problem. My first instinct was to focus on colours, layout and typography, but after analysing the SQLite website in detail, I realised that its main weaknesses were navigation, accessibility and how information was organised. That changed the direction of the project. Instead of replacing the site's identity, I kept its technical style while restructuring the content around common user tasks, improving accessibility, and making important information easier to find. Throughout development I also used automated checks to catch issues such as contrast problems and accessibility regressions, then fixed them before moving on. This made the redesign much more systematic than simply changing the appearance.

## 2. What did this work change about who you want to be as a software developer?

This project reinforced that good software development is about making deliberate decisions rather than making everything look different. Every design change needed a clear reason that I could explain and defend. I also learned the value of defining a realistic scope. Instead of trying to redesign every SQLite documentation page, I focused on representative pages and linked to the official site where appropriate. Going forward, I want to become the kind of developer who understands the existing strengths of a system, improves the parts that genuinely need improvement, and uses testing and evidence to support design decisions instead of relying on personal preference.
